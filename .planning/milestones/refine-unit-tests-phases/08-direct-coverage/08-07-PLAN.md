not close.

`orchestrators/plugin/install-outcome.ts` reads 75 lines, 23 branches, and 5 functions short
against its own owner test. It is a split casualty, not untested code: run the sibling
`tests/orchestrators/plugin/install-flow.test.ts` against the same module and all but six of the
uncovered runs cover. The split moved the module out and left its tests behind. That makes most
of this work relocation — adding owner cases that drive `runInstallLedger` directly — rather than
inventing behavior.

Three of those six remaining runs are the `commitPrepared*` leak arms: `commitPreparedSkills`,
then Commands, then Agents, each returning a leak string only when `cleanupStaging` fails, each
pushing it onto `bridgeWarnings`. They are uncovered even under the sibling suite, because nothing
could make a cleanup fail without patching a builtin. Plan 05 made `removalOps` a required member
of `InstallLedgerOptions`, so these three arms are now reachable from the owner test. The port and
this shortfall were one problem.

`D-08-A06` is explicit about the fallback: this module is **not** eligible for a pin row. Seventy-
five uncovered lines across nineteen disjoint runs have no single recorded reason, and `D-08-07`
requires one per row. If the work cannot finish inside the phase, that is an escalation to the
developer. The residue research could not size — the preflight throw arms, the `resolveGitPluginRoot`
resolver callback, the `hooksPhase.do` body, and the `statePhase` record assembly — is the one
item in this phase whose effort is not yet bounded, which is why the plan carries a blocking
checkpoint rather than a promise.

Purpose: give the owner pair an honest complete reading, or an honest recorded shortfall with a
named next step.
Output: owner cases for every reachable uncovered run, the three leak arms closed through the
port, and either a complete reading or a recorded escalation.
