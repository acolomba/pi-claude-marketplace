Pin the precedence rule the resolver now evaluates, in both resolution modes and
in both directions, plus the two DFEN-01 guards that live at resolution time
rather than at schema time.

The rule itself already shipped in plan 101-01 as a single private helper. What is
missing is the evidence that it means what the requirement says — in particular the
entry-`true`-beats-manifest-`false` direction, which nothing about the false-wins

`plugin.json` must NOT be treated as the declaration conflict that a component key
in the same position would be.

cheap to assert and expensive to get wrong later. Pinning mode parity here is what
lets the read-surface phase render one answer without explaining a warm-versus-cold
divergence it never had.
Output: the strict-mode matrix, the loose-mode non-conflict and parity proofs, and
the two resolution-time validation guards.

Surfaced by the edge probe and deliberately left UNRESOLVED rather than

- **DFEN-02 — edge category `unclassified`.** The probe emitted three classified
  rows (`adjacency`, `empty`, `ordering`) attributed to DFEN-01, whose content
  actually describes DFEN-02's precedence semantics, and left DFEN-02 itself
  unclassified with no predicate. The planner did not re-attribute the resolved
  rows to make the tally look tidy: their predicates are authored above as explicit
  truths and as the agreement, fallback and parity cases in both tasks, where the
  behavior lives. What stays unresolved is the DFEN-02 probe row itself. A reviewer
  who wants a shape predicate specifically for it should ask about the axis the
  taxonomy has no category for — what happens when the two sites disagree AND one
  of them is structurally unreadable. This phase answers that by never reaching
  precedence at all: a `plugin.json` that fails validation short-circuits to
  `unavailable` inside `preflightStages` before the helper is called, so the
  disagreement never materializes. That behavior is pinned by the malformed-manifest
  guard in Task 1, but as a validation case rather than as a precedence case.
