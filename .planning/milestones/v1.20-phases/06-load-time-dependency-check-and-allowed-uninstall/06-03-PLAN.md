<objective>
Stop `uninstall` refusing to remove a plugin that other installed plugins in the
scope still declare. It proceeds, names the dependents on the success row, and
each of them is reported unsatisfied at the next load by the check plans 06-01
and 06-02 built. The refusal and its vocabulary are retired rather than left
dead.

Purpose: LOAD-03 is only safe once LOAD-01 exists — that is why this phase leads
with the check and follows with the allowed uninstall. Removing the refusal
before the check existed would have turned a blocked mistake into a silent one.
With the check in place the dependents are visibly reported instead, which is
upstream's behaviour and removes the two-stale-records deadlock the current
refusal can produce.

Output: an uninstall that proceeds and reports, a retired refusal token, a new
success-row token, and the documentation and fixtures that pin both.
</objective>

  <name>Task 1: Confirm the two one-way decisions before the refusal is removed</name>

  <decision>
Two decisions in `06-CONTEXT.md` are rated one-way, and both are executed by
this plan. Neither is cheap to reverse once shipped, so they are confirmed
before the work rather than discovered after it.

**D-06-06 — `uninstall` reports instead of refusing.** The dependent set is
still computed exactly as it is today, by the same offline declaration walk
inside the same locked transaction. What changes is the outcome: a non-empty
dependent set stops blocking the removal and becomes an informational reason on
the ordinary success row. Rated one-way because it publishes a catalog row, and
a catalog row's bytes are pinned and then depended on.

**D-06-07 — the refusal and its vocabulary are RETIRED, not repurposed.** The
alternative was to keep the existing refusal token and re-point it at the new
success row. The arithmetic is identical either way — the closed set lands at 58
members — but the diff is not: repurposing keeps roughly fifteen of the
seventeen affected sites as comment-only edits, where retiring makes most of them
real edits. Retiring is what `06-CONTEXT.md` D-06-07 and the roadmap's success
criterion 4 both say, and it is the right call on the merits: the token's
current comment block states that the operation was refused and nothing was
removed, and that sentence becomes false. A reader who greps the token would be
actively misled by the cheaper option.

**What proceeding means.** After this plan, a user who runs
`uninstall <plugin>` on something another installed plugin declares gets the
removal, not a refusal. The dependents are named on the row and each is disabled
with a remedy at the next reload. The behaviour is upstream's, and it removes
the deadlock where two plugins declaring each other can never be removed.

**What is NOT changing, and is worth confirming explicitly.** The fail-closed
refusal on an unreadable declarer SURVIVES. If the command cannot establish who
declares the target, it still refuses. Only the found-dependents outcome moves
from refuse to proceed.
  </decision>

  <options>
    - **proceed-as-decided** — retire the refusal and its token, add the new
      success-row token, keep the unreadable-declarer refusal. This is what
      `06-CONTEXT.md` and the roadmap both specify.

  </options>
